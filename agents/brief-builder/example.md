# Brief Builder — example

A realistic run. All names are fictional. This is the "Allergy Parents" study
that threads through every agent's example in this kit — here it is at stage
1, as it actually arrives: messy.

## What the user pasted in

> Here's everything I have on the new Meridian Foods project. Turn it into a
> brief.
>
> **Email from client (Tuesday):**
> "Following up on the call — we want to talk to parents of kids with food
> allergies, US only. Thinking 300 completes? Survey's about 15 minutes.
> Marketing wants read-outs by age of child so let's make sure we get younger
> and older kids. We'd love this before the QBR on the 24th. Budget is tight
> this quarter so keep sample costs down."
>
> **My kickoff call notes:**
> - n=400 (Dana said 300 in email but on the call we agreed 400 for the
>   age cuts to hold up)
> - child age bands: 0–5 / 6–12 / 13–17, want readable cells
> - "diagnosed" food allergy — Dana kept saying diagnosed, check if
>   self-diagnosed counts
> - Dana figures maybe 1 in 5 parents deal with this?? seems high
> - we host in Forsta, LOI estimated not tested
> - QBR is the 24th, so field needs to close ~21st → about 10 field days
>   if we launch on the 11th

## What the Brief Builder returned

*(After the user answered two clarifying questions: the deadline is hard —
the QBR won't move — and "diagnosed" means diagnosed by a healthcare
professional.)*

```
SAMPLE BRIEF — Meridian Foods / Allergy Parents study

Audience: US parents of children under 18 who have been diagnosed with a
  food allergy by a healthcare professional.
Qualification criteria:
  - Parent or legal guardian of at least one child under 18 living in
    their household
  - At least one such child has a food allergy diagnosed by a healthcare
    professional (self-diagnosis does not qualify)
  - Resident of the United States
Exclusions: none stated — ASSUMPTION: standard industry/sensitive-
  occupation exclusions to be confirmed
Market(s) & languages: US, English — ASSUMPTION: English only; confirm
  whether Spanish-language completes are wanted
Completes (n): 400
Assumed IR: 20% (basis: client guess — "maybe 1 in 5 parents." Not
  validated; user's own notes flag it as feeling high)
LOI: 15 min (estimated, not tested)
Quotas: by age of allergic child — 0–5 / 6–12 / 13–17. Targets not yet
  set; client wants "readable cells." Not interlocked with anything else.
Device: ASSUMPTION: must be mobile-friendly (consumer parent audience)
Timeline: launch June 11, close June 21 — deadline is HARD (client QBR
  June 24)
Survey hosting: buyer-hosted (Forsta)
Incentives: MISSING — assume supplier-handled unless told otherwise
Sensitivities: children's health topic — expect supplier screening rules
  around interviewing about minors; budget pressure noted by client

GAPS & ASSUMPTIONS
- ASSUMPTION: the 20% IR is a client guess. If true IR is materially
  lower, cost and timeline change substantially. Flag this in the RFP and
  ask suppliers for their estimate.
- MISSING: quota targets per age band. "Readable cells" at n=400 implies
  roughly 130+ per band — decide before RFP.
- MISSING: incentive handling; language policy.

CONTRADICTIONS NOTICED
- n: client email says 300; call notes say 400 agreed on the call. Brief
  uses 400 — confirm with Dana in writing before RFP.

BEFORE THIS GOES TO SUPPLIERS, DECIDE:
1. Confirm n=400 with the client in writing (email trail says 300).
2. Set the three age-band quota targets (even splits ~133 each, or
   weighted?).
3. Confirm English-only vs. +Spanish.
4. Decide whether a child's allergy "diagnosed by an HCP" will be
   screener-verifiable as worded, or whether you'll accept parent report
   of a diagnosis. (This wording drives IR and data quality.)
```

## Why this example matters

Notice what the agent did **not** do: it didn't accept 20% IR as fact, didn't
quietly resolve the 300-vs-400 contradiction, and didn't estimate what the
project would cost. Every judgment call is visible and assigned to a human.
This brief is now ready for the [Feasibility Checker](../feasibility-checker/).
