# Full workflow example — one project, start to finish

The "Allergy Parents" study runs through every agent in this kit. Each
agent's `example.md` shows its stage in full detail; this doc is the
connected story. Client, suppliers, and people are all fictional.

## The project

Meridian Foods wants to survey **US parents of children under 18 with an
HCP-diagnosed food allergy** — n=400, ~15-minute survey, results needed for
a client QBR in under a month. It arrives, as projects do, as one client
email and a page of call notes.

## Stage 1 — Brief Builder

**In:** the email + call notes.
**Out:** a structured brief — and three catches: the email said n=300 while
the call notes said 400 (flagged, not silently resolved); the 20% IR was
labeled as a client guess needing validation; quota targets and language
policy were surfaced as unmade decisions.
**Human moves:** confirmed n=400 in writing, set quotas at ~133 per child
age band (0–5 / 6–12 / 13–17), English-only.
→ [Full detail](../agents/brief-builder/example.md)

## Stage 2 — Feasibility Checker

**In:** the confirmed brief.
**Out:** overall risk **Medium-High**, concentrated in one interaction:
a guessed 20% IR (published child food-allergy prevalence ~8% says it's
optimistic) under a hard 10-day window. Mitigations: ask suppliers for
*their* IR estimates in the RFP, mandate a day-1 soft launch with a
pre-agreed contingency, get per-cell feasibility on 13–17.
**Human moves:** adopted all three, pre-agreed a fallback with the client
(if IR reads <10% at soft launch: extend teen cell or accept it under
target).
→ [Full detail](../agents/feasibility-checker/example.md)

*(Sidebar: this is also where the kit's cross-cutting seventh agent, the
[Incentive Advisor](../agents/incentive-advisor/), earns its keep on B2B
projects — set the respondent incentive before the RFP so bids come back
against a real number. For this consumer study the team used the supplier's
norm; the Incentive Advisor's own example shows why it declines to
salary-anchor consumer audiences.)*

## Stage 3 — RFP Drafter

**In:** the brief + the feasibility review's supplier questions.
**Out:** a detailed RFP with ten numbered response requirements — including
"what happens to price and timeline if actual IR is materially lower than
your assumption" — a short version, and follow-ups. No budget number
anywhere in it.
**Human moves:** sent the identical spec to three suppliers.
→ [Full detail](../agents/rfp-drafter/example.md)

## Stage 4 — Bid Comparator

**In:** three replies, pasted raw. PanelCo: $11.50 on an evidence-based 15%
IR, bounded worst case. SampleWorks: $8.25 on an unexplained 22% IR,
"pricing subject to revision," silent on removal costs. FieldBridge: $13.75
on a conservative 10% IR they defend, strongest commitments, tiered
*downward* pricing if IR beats it.
**Out:** the normalized table (SampleWorks' column full of NOT STATED), the
"these prices aren't comparable — they're different bets" analysis, and
pre-award questions per supplier. No winner declared.
**Human moves:** the researcher — whose stated priority was feasibility
confidence — chose FieldBridge after getting its below-10% terms in
writing.
→ [Full detail](../agents/bid-comparator/example.md)

## Stage 5 — Questionnaire Risk Reviewer

**In:** the draft questionnaire, four days before launch.
**Out:** the killer finding — S1 asked "Do you have a child diagnosed with
a food allergy?" as question one, telegraphing the qual to every fraudster
and yes-sayer. Plus: LOI arithmetic reading 15–20 min against the 15-min
claim, a quota-variable gap for multi-child households, a grid fatigue
cliff, and zero attention checks. "The one change that matters most": fix
S1 with a concealed condition list.
**Human moves:** restructured the screener, added two attention checks,
made the long open-end optional, told FieldBridge to re-confirm its IR
estimate against the new wording.
→ [Full detail](../agents/questionnaire-risk-reviewer/example.md)

## Stage 6 — Fielding Risk Advisor

**In:** the day-4 update. Topline healthy: 178/400, IR reading 11% (better
than bought), pace ahead of plan.
**Out:** trajectory risk **Medium-High** anyway — the 13–17 cell sat at
24/133, needing ~18/day against a demonstrated 6/day, while the easy cells
were about to hit their caps. Decide-now: trigger-check the pre-agreed
fallback *today*; cap the overfilling cells. Plus supplier questions with
numbers in them, including the polite version of "you said you'd field this
cell first."
**Human moves:** capped the two easy cells, got FieldBridge to commit to a
teen-cell daily rate in writing, and warned the client the 13–17 read might
land at ~110–120 completes — three days before it would have been a
surprise.
→ [Full detail](../agents/fielding-risk-advisor/example.md)

## The point

Six stages, six small interventions, each one cheap at the moment it
happened and expensive a week later. No agent predicted a price, certified
feasibility, or made a decision — every catch was surfaced early enough for
a human to act on it. That's the kit working as intended.
