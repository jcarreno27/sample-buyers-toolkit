# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used across this kit. If you're new to
buying sample, read [sample-procurement-basics.md](sample-procurement-basics.md)
first — it puts these terms in order.

**Sample** — The people who take your survey. "Buying sample" means paying a
supplier for access to respondents who match your target audience.

**Panel** — A supplier's pool of pre-recruited people who have agreed to take
surveys. Suppliers may also pull from partner panels or exchanges (see blend).

**Respondent** — One person taking your survey.

**n / sample size** — The number of completed interviews you need (e.g., n=400).

**Complete** — A respondent who qualified, finished the survey, and passed
quality checks. You typically pay per complete.

**IR (incidence rate)** — The percentage of people who enter your survey and
qualify for it. A 20% IR means 1 in 5 entrants qualify. IR is the single
biggest driver of cost and feasibility, and the number most often assumed
optimistically. Always pin down the **base**: 20% of general-population
entrants and 20% of a pre-targeted frame (e.g., parents only) are wildly
different projects, and practitioners quote IR against different
denominators without saying so.

**LOI (length of interview)** — How long the survey takes to complete, in
minutes. Longer LOI means higher cost, more dropout, and worse data quality —
especially on mobile.

**CPI (cost per interview / cost per complete)** — What you pay the supplier
per complete. Driven mainly by IR, LOI, audience rarity, market, quotas, and
timeline.

**Quota** — A required number of completes within a subgroup (e.g., 100
completes per region). Quotas make fielding harder than the topline n suggests.

**Interlocked quotas** — Quotas that must be met in combination (e.g., age ×
gender × region simultaneously). Difficulty multiplies; the last few cells are
always the hardest to fill.

**Feasibility** — Whether a supplier can realistically deliver your n, in your
market, at your IR, within your timeline. Always an estimate, never a
guarantee.

**Screener** — The questions at the start of a survey that determine whether a
respondent qualifies. Screener design directly affects IR, fraud risk, and
quota fill.

**Concealed-list screener** — A screener pattern that hides the qualifying
condition inside a list of plausible options ("Which of the following has a
doctor diagnosed any of your children with?" with decoys) instead of asking
for it directly. The direct ask telegraphs how to qualify; the concealed
list keeps measured IR meaningful and raises the cost of fraud.

**Quota gate** — The point in the survey flow where a qualified respondent
is checked against quota cells (and terminated as over-quota if their cell
is full). Every variable a quota needs must be captured before this point.

**Attention check / consistency check** — Quality questions embedded in a
survey: an attention check has one obviously correct answer ("select
'somewhat agree' for this row"); a consistency check re-asks a fact from
earlier in different words. Too few invites junk data; too many purges
genuine respondents.

**Fatigue cliff** — The point in a survey where respondent effort collapses
— typically after a streak of grids or repeated near-identical batteries.
Drop-off and straightlining spike past it.

**Quals / qualifications** — The criteria a respondent must meet (e.g., "parent
of a child under 18 with a diagnosed food allergy").

**Fielding / in field** — The period when the survey is live and collecting
completes.

**Soft launch** — Releasing the survey to a small slice first (often ~10% of
sample) to check IR, LOI, and data quality before full launch. Standard
practice; skipping it is a risk flag.

**Drop-off / abandon rate** — The share of respondents who start but don't
finish. Rises with LOI, grids, and mobile-hostile design.

**Speeder** — A respondent who finishes implausibly fast. Usually removed in
quality checks.

**Straightliner** — A respondent who gives the same answer down an entire grid.
Usually removed.

**Bot / fraud / dupe** — Automated, fraudulent, or duplicate respondents.
Suppliers run detection; high fraud removal rates can also signal a screener
that telegraphs how to qualify.

**Blend / sample blend** — Completes sourced from more than one panel or from
an exchange/marketplace rather than a single proprietary panel. Not inherently
bad, but you should know what you're getting.

**Exchange / marketplace sample** — Sample sourced programmatically from many
panels through a platform. Fast and scalable; consistency and transparency
vary.

**River sample** — Respondents recruited in the moment (e.g., via ads) rather
than from a panel. Useful for low-IR or hard-to-panel audiences.

**B2B / B2C / HCP** — Business audiences (often job-title qualified), consumer
audiences, and healthcare professionals. B2B and HCP sample is scarcer and
much more expensive, with higher fraud incentive.

**RFP (request for proposal)** — Your request to suppliers describing the
project and asking for a quote and feasibility statement.

**Bid / quote** — A supplier's response: price (CPI or total), feasibility,
timeline, and assumptions. The assumptions are the part people under-read.

**IR adjustment clause** — Contract/quote language saying the CPI changes if
actual IR differs from assumed IR. This is where "cheap" bids get expensive.

**Reconciliation** — The end-of-project accounting of completes delivered vs.
billed, removals, and over-quotas.

**Over-quota (OQ)** — A respondent who qualifies but whose quota cell is
already full. High OQ rates waste sample and annoy suppliers' panelists.

**Device split** — The share of respondents on mobile vs. desktop. Most
consumer sample is majority-mobile; surveys must work on a phone.

**Close-out** — The final push to fill remaining quota cells and end fielding.

**DIY platform** — Self-serve sample-buying tools. This kit's advice applies
there too — you just play both buyer and PM.
